## 2026-11-04

- 01:27 PST: Following last night's planned step, tonight runs Exp 6, which scores recovery on the variables an agent would regulate (energy, cumulative intake, prediction error) instead of hidden-state distance. It reuses the Exp 5 forks (step 2500) and the same matched targets, 7 variants × 3 seeds, 40 targets per run, 120 steps. There are two arms. (a) Hidden-state kicks (ε 0.1, 0.3) are scored on |ΔE|, Δintake and ΔPE against a world-chaos noise floor from unperturbed twins with a different weather/birth PRNG key. (b) An energy-drop test removes 50% of the target's energy and compares the closed loop with a passive control whose actions are clamped to the unperturbed twin's actions, plus a sensor-blind control whose energy sense reads as if nothing was lost. Active regulation means the closed loop closes the deficit more than the clamped body. New `sim6.py` is `sim5` plus an observer-side intervention hook (ctl=None is sim5 exactly; a clamp to the agent's own actions reproduced the reference bit for bit in a smoke test). Running detached as `mvp/run6_chain.sh` with per-run checkpoints in `runs6/`.

- 01:41 PST: A check on the first finished runs caught a flaw in the passive control. The sim zeroes an agent's recorded actions on the step it dies, so after the reference agent dies the clamped twin is replaying zeros, not real behaviour. That broke the clamp-to-own-actions check over 120 steps, even though it was exact while the reference lived. The analysis now uses only targets whose unperturbed reference survives all 120 steps. That condition depends only on the unperturbed run, so it's the same for every arm (about half of targets qualify). Mean recovery fractions are very noisy, because reproduction halves an agent's energy. So "active" now also requires that most targets end with a smaller deficit in the closed loop (CI on that fraction above 0.5) and that the mean-difference CI excludes 0. This makes the rule stricter, never looser. First look at the main arm (2 of 3 seeds) shows no difference: median A ≈ 0.00, and the closed loop wins on 47% of targets.

- 01:51 PST: Interim results with all 3 seeds for the main arm (sw_pecost_pe) and sw_full; the other 5 variants are still computing. After the 50% energy drop, the main arm's closed loop recovers *less* than the clamped passive body: A = −0.28 [−0.43, −0.12]. Per target it's a coin flip (closed loop wins 47% [43%, 50%], median A ≈ −0.01), so this is at best "passive ≥ active", not regulation. sw_full shows no difference (A = +0.08 [−0.83, +0.99]). Blinding the energy sensor changes nothing measurable. Hidden-state kicks move energy less than the world-noise floor (kick 0.1 |ΔE| ≈ 0.15–0.18 vs twin-world ≈ 0.29–0.36 over steps 60–120). At the level of energy, then, the hidden-state amplification seen in Exp 3–5 is small compared with ordinary weather chaos. Caveat: the twin floor changes the whole world's weather, so it's an upper bound on world noise, not a matched one.
